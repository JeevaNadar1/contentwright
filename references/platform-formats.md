# Platform formats

Read only the sections in play. Each platform rewards a different shape. Posting the same text everywhere is the fastest way to look automated.

Contents:
1. LinkedIn
2. YouTube long form
3. Shorts, Reels, TikTok
4. X posts and threads
5. Newsletter
6. Length targets

---

## 1. LinkedIn

The first two lines are the whole game. Everything after the "see more" fold only gets read if those lines earn it.

Structure:

1. Hook, one line, under 12 words. No context, no throat clearing.
2. Second line that pays off enough of the hook to buy the click.
3. Body, one idea, broken into one and two line paragraphs.
4. The turn. The line where the reader's assumption breaks.
5. Close. The sharpest sentence, or a question that is actually answerable in a comment.

Rules:
1. No links in the body if reach matters. Put them in the first comment and say so.
2. Three hashtags maximum, or none. None is usually better.
3. Line breaks do the work punctuation does elsewhere. Use white space deliberately.
4. A list post needs a reason to be a list. Five items with one line each is a list. Five items with a paragraph each is an article in disguise.

Formats worth rotating:
1. **Story with a cost.** Something went wrong, it cost something real, here is the rule you now follow.
2. **Contrarian take.** Name the common advice, name who it fails, give the replacement.
3. **Teardown.** Take something public and explain why it works or does not.
4. **Receipts.** A number, then the method behind it.
5. **Behind the scenes.** What the work actually looks like on a Tuesday.
6. **Correction.** A thing you believed and stopped believing, with the moment it changed.

## 2. YouTube long form

Retention is decided in the first 30 seconds and then again at every transition.

Script structure:

1. **Cold open, 0 to 15 seconds.** Start inside the action. The most surprising sentence in the script goes here. No welcome, no channel name, no "in this video we will".
2. **Stakes, 15 to 45 seconds.** Why this matters to them, specifically. Name the cost of not knowing.
3. **Promise and shape, under 15 seconds.** What they will be able to do by the end. Keep it concrete.
4. **Beat map.** Three to six beats. Each beat is a claim, evidence, and a payoff. Open each beat with a line that could survive as a standalone clip.
5. **The turn.** One beat that reverses expectation. Place it around 60 percent through, where drop-off usually spikes.
6. **Close.** The takeaway in one sentence, then the CTA. One CTA only.

Deliver alongside the script:
1. Three title options, under 60 characters, each with a different angle.
2. Thumbnail text, three words maximum.
3. Description, two to three sentences plus timestamps.
4. Chapter markers by beat.

Writing for the ear:
1. Sentences shorter than you would write them. A speaker runs out of breath where a reader does not.
2. Repeat the key term instead of using pronouns. Listeners lose the thread faster than readers.
3. Mark pauses and emphasis in brackets only where delivery is not obvious.
4. Never write a sentence with three subordinate clauses. Nobody can say it.

## 3. Shorts, Reels, TikTok

One idea. 30 to 60 seconds. No setup.

1. **First 2 seconds.** The claim, spoken, with no preamble. The visual should already be moving.
2. **Next 10 seconds.** Why the claim is not obvious.
3. **Middle.** One example. One. A second example kills the pacing.
4. **Last line.** Either a hard stop on the strongest phrase or a question that earns a comment.

Never open with "hey guys", never open with a logo, never explain what the video is about before doing it.

## 4. X posts and threads

Single post: one claim, under 280 characters, no hashtags, no thread bait. If it needs context, it is a thread.

Thread structure:
1. Post one carries the whole value on its own. If it only promises value, it fails.
2. One claim per post. No post should contain two ideas.
3. No numbering unless the order matters.
4. No "a thread" label and no emoji bullets.
5. Last post is the strongest line, not a summary and not a follow-me plea. If a CTA is required, keep it to one short line under the payoff.

Threads work when each post could be screenshotted alone. Test each one against that.

## 5. Newsletter

The one place where length is allowed, so use it for the thing that did not fit anywhere else.

1. Subject line, under 45 characters, specific, no curiosity gap tricks that the body does not pay off.
2. First line visible in preview. It is a second subject line. Do not waste it on a greeting.
3. One argument per issue. Sections only if the argument has parts.
4. Personal voice higher than any other channel. This is the inbox, not a feed.
5. One CTA at the end. Two CTAs halve both.

---

## 6. Length targets

| Format | Target | Hard ceiling |
|---|---|---|
| LinkedIn post | 120 to 200 words | 300 words |
| LinkedIn story post | 200 to 280 words | 350 words |
| X single | under 280 characters | 280 characters |
| X thread | 5 to 9 posts | 12 posts |
| YouTube long form | 900 to 1600 words, roughly 6 to 11 minutes | 2500 words |
| Short or Reel | 90 to 150 words | 180 words |
| Newsletter | 500 to 900 words | 1400 words |

Going under target is usually fine. Going over means an idea was not decided on.
