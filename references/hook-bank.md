# Hook bank

A hook is not a genre of sentence. It is a promise that the next line has to keep. Write three for every piece and lead with the best one. The other two go in the notes so the user can swap after testing.

## The test

A hook works when the reader cannot guess the next line but wants to. It fails when it could sit on top of any post in the category. If swapping the topic out still leaves the hook intact, it is decoration, not a hook.

## Openings that work

**The number that does not add up**
"We spent 11 months building a feature 4 people used."
"Three of my five best clients came from the same rejected pitch."

Use when: the user has real figures. Strongest opening available. Never fabricate the number.

**The flat contradiction**
"Hiring senior people slowed us down."
"Our best month came after we cut the ad spend to zero."

Use when: the user holds a position most of their field does not. Must be defended in the body or it reads as bait.

**The moment**
"The client asked one question and I had no answer."
"I found the bug at 2am, in a file nobody had opened in a year."

Use when: there is a real story. Start inside the scene, never before it.

**The cost**
"That mistake cost us [amount]."
"Two years of work, gone in one meeting."

Use when: the lesson was expensive. The cost must be named specifically.

**The correction**
"I was wrong about pricing for six years."
"Everything I taught about this in 2023 is out of date."

Use when: the user changed their mind. Rare, and it earns trust faster than anything else.

**The instruction**
"Stop writing your own job descriptions."
"Delete the second follow-up email."

Use when: the advice is unusual enough that the reader wants the reason.

**The named observation**
"Every failing project I have seen had the same status update."
"There is a specific week where every new hire either clicks or quits."

Use when: the user has pattern recognition from volume. Pays off only if the pattern is then named exactly.

**The overheard line**
"My accountant said something last week that I have not stopped thinking about."

Use sparingly. Once per batch at most, or it turns into a tic.

## Openings to avoid

1. Any rhetorical question. "Ever wondered", "What if I told you", "Have you noticed".
2. Definition openers. "Marketing is the art of".
3. Context before content. "In today's competitive market".
4. Announcing the format. "Here are 5 lessons", "A thread on".
5. Credentials first. "As someone who has spent 15 years".
6. Greetings of any kind.

## Second lines

The second line decides whether the hook was honest. Three moves that work:

1. **Deepen the cost.** Hook says it went wrong, line two says how badly.
2. **Refuse the obvious explanation.** "It was not a skill problem."
3. **Jump the timeline.** "That was March. By June the whole team had gone."

Never use line two to restate line one in different words. That is where most posts lose the reader.

## Endings

1. **Flat stop.** End on the sharpest sentence and leave. Strongest ending for a strong post.
2. **Callback.** Return to the hook's image with the new meaning it now has.
3. **Answerable question.** Not "what do you think", which invites nothing. "What is the earliest you have caught this?" invites a story.
4. **Single instruction.** One thing to go do today, stated in under eight words.
5. **The honest gap.** Say what you still have not figured out. Draws people who know more than you into the comments.

Never end with a summary, a hashtag block, a thank you, or two CTAs.

## Hook counts by job

1. Single post: 3 hooks.
2. Batch of 5: 1 hook each, plus 1 alternate for the two strongest pieces.
3. Video script: 3 cold opens, because the cold open and the title have to work together.
4. Thread: 3 versions of post one. Everything else follows from it.
